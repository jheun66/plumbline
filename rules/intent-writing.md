# Writing intent

The lead writes intent with the user and confirms it with the user. The form of intent is the project's own ([layers.md](layers.md#what-each-project-decides)). A confirmed intent is changed through a change ([change.md](change.md)).

## Marks

Every item of a draft carries one of three marks:

| Mark | Means |
|---|---|
| decided | the user said or chose it |
| proposed | the lead's thought, which the user has not decided yet |
| open | still open |

- Near its top, the draft names the three marks in the project's own words.
- The decided mark may be left out, when the draft says that an item with no mark is decided.
- Marks are used only in drafts. They are never carried into documents of other layers.

## The confirmed mark

When intent is confirmed, its top says, in the project's own words, that the user confirmed it, and on which date.

## Steps

1. The lead talks with the user. The user may answer first in their own words, or the lead may listen and draft. The order is not fixed; the conversation goes both ways.
2. The draft is written at the intent location. That location is decided first ([layers.md](layers.md#when-settings-are-decided)).
3. Of what intent must say, which is the goals and scope, the milestones, and the decisions and their reasons, whatever the user has not said is marked open and asked.
4. Open items are resolved one at a time. When the user answers, the item is decided.
5. What follows directly from earlier decisions is written as decided, without asking, and the user is told. When the user objects, it becomes open again.
6. When the user asks for the lead's thought, the lead gives it with its reasons and with what it is less sure of, and the draft holds it as proposed. When the user accepts or changes it, it is decided.
7. When nothing proposed or open is left, the user reads the whole. When the user understands it and says it is right, it is confirmed, and the lead writes the confirmed mark.

## What always holds

- The lead never fills what the user has not said with a default of its own.
- What the lead asks the user is in plain words, with no name left unexplained. When the user wants, the lead shows a concrete example.
- What each project decides, such as its language or its code comment policy, is not asked about separately. When the user states it, it goes into intent.
- Intent is confirmed only when nothing proposed or open is left, and the user has read and understood the whole.
